# REMOTE / issue #44 — local implementation

Committed on `main` on top of `5336965`.

The remote droid slot is now built for every carrying class instead of being left out. A press of the remote weapon throws the class's droid entity through the dispenser's launch (charge-scaled `Velocity`, the owner's velocity added) and hands the player's controls to it: the soldier stands still while the stick flies the droid, the look stick turns and pitches it within `PitchLimits`, FIRE shoots its blaster (the crosshair follows the droid's aim), and one press of the secondary trigger starts its destruct's 3 s countdown. ACTION gives control back and leaves the droid hovering where it is; a fresh press of the remote's trigger takes it again, and no second droid is thrown while one lives. The droid runs down on its `AddHealth` drain (BF1 40 s) or BF2's `EnergyAutoRestore` drain (about 58.8 s), can be shot and blasted by `DroidScale`, and on its end explodes with its class's `ExplosionName`, returning control to the soldier.

The flight step is BF1's `FUN_001c9fd8`: a lift spring over a ground ray of ten times `SetAltitude`, gravity applied before the damping reads the velocity (so it settles 0.09 m above the authored altitude at 30 Hz, as the original does), and stick drive toward `MaxSpeed` / `MaxStrafeSpeed` under the acceleration cap scaled by ground contact. BF1 reads only the misspelled `Acceleraton`, so its droid keeps the constructor's 70 even though the disc authors 1.0; BF2 reads the authored 25. The hover-style keys on the disc (`ForwardSpeed`, `turnRate`, …) are ignored, as the original's class setter ignores them.

The countdown cannot be cancelled and runs whether or not the trigger is held, as both originals' destruct predicates do. The destruct is BF1's orbital salvo (4 rounds, 0.75 s apart, from 600 m at 70° with a 2.5 m spread, each a 1000-damage bolt that explodes for 500 over 10.5 m, all credited to the owner) and BF2's incinerator burst (10000 damage over 12 m, people / droids / animals only, plus its sound).

Bots launch a droid when they have an enemy target within 40 m and the remote is ready, steer at the nearest visible enemy, hold their distance around the blaster's `OptimalRange`, fire within `MaxRange`, and hand back after 10 s without a target. That behaviour is ours: the BF1 AI's use of the remote was not read, and BF2's weapon suitability only shows that the remote is always a candidate.

The windowed game's render blend (card RINT) draws a droid between its ticks, matched by its id, and its per-frame mouse-look preview turns a flown droid the way the next tick will, so its camera and aim move at the display's rate as the soldier's do. Droids, their weapon state and salvo runs are in the deterministic sim hash. The game draws the `com_weap_probedroid` model, follows the droid with its `TrackOffset` / `EyePointOffset` camera, shows its health and energy on the HUD with the reticle, plays the charge sound, the droid's explosion and the BF2 burst sound, and spawns their effects. The headless event log records the charge and the droid's end.

## Coverage

The data integration test (`game/tests/suite/remote_droids.rs`) checks the disc census: **BF1 8 classes, BF2 13 classes**. For every carrying class it builds the slot (channel 1, 1 clip), checks the droid, blaster and destruct values against the disc (altitude, acceleration, limits, springs, health, drain, camera offset, blaster delay / velocity / damage, the salvo or burst numbers), loads the droid model, then launches, flies 2 s forward with the soldier standing still, hands back with ACTION, and checks the droid expires at its lifetime ±0.2 s.

Focused tests: 7 in `core/src/remote/tests.rs` (BF1's misspelled acceleration, the gravity-first hover offset, stick to MaxSpeed along the heading, no steering out of the ground ray's reach, drain lifetime in both games, salvo geometry, the hand-built rotations against glam) and 9 in `core/src/sim/remotes/tests.rs` (launch / steer / hand back / retake in both games, run-down with no second throw, blaster kills credited to the owner, damage by DroidScale, one press counting down to the salvo, the launch and retake presses not starting it, the look preview matching the next tick, a bot's launch and fire, replay hashing), 1 in `game/src/walk/tests.rs` (the crosshair stays on screen and follows the flown droid's pitch), 1 in `game/src/render_blend.rs` (a droid drawn between its ticks and turned by the preview), and a second data test that taps the destruct on a real BF1 marksman and BF2 officer and sees it 3.0 s later.

The class-kit expectation changes exactly the 21 formerly omitted remote slots to `remote droid x1`, and the reason on Chewbacca's guided-rocket slot (a different `remote` weapon, still not built).

## Remaining approximations

- [H] ACTION is the hand-back button and a dead owner ends the droid; the original's second update byte (+0x74) that kills its own droid was not identified, so it is not bound.
- [H] The BF2 burst follows the normal blast friendly-fire rule; the emitter's `MaxTargets` 10 and 120° cone are not modelled.
- [H] BF2's energy starts at 100.
- OURS: the bot behaviour, the pitch rate (`MaxTurnSpeed`), the 0.35 m collision sphere, the flat frame normal (the original's (n.x k, n.y + 0.1, n.z k) shaping and its velocity ray are omitted).
- The engine and radio loops (`remotedroid_engine_lp`, `remotedroid_radio`) are not played; BF2's alternate charge camera (0x83e12771 / 0xe11ce401) and charge effect are not shown.
- Enemy bots do not pick a droid as a target; they hit it only incidentally.
